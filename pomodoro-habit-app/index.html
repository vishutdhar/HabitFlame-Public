A Pomodoro timer inside your habit tracker

Some habits need a box of time, not a checkbox. HabitFlame keeps a Pomodoro timer one tap away on the Today tab, so reading, studying or deep work gets its 25 minutes, and you log the minutes on your habit when you are done.

Why a timer and a tracker belong together

People usually run two apps: one that counts the streak, one that counts the minutes. Switching between them is where the habit slips. You finish a focus session in one app and forget to log it in the other, or you tick the habit without doing the work. Keeping the timer and the tracker in the same app shrinks that gap to a few seconds.

In HabitFlame the focus timer opens from a button on the Today tab. The timer and your habits stay separate: the timer keeps the time, and when you finish, you log the minutes on your duration habit yourself. Logging it yourself means you record what you actually did, and the streak, the stats and, with Premium, your accountability partner all see the same completion.

How the timer works

The timer follows the classic Pomodoro rhythm: a 25 minute work session, then a 5 minute short break, with a 15 minute long break after a set of four sessions. The screen shows the time remaining and which phase you are in, and a row of dots shows how many sessions of the set you have completed. Breaks are timed too, so you come back on time instead of drifting.

Start a session and switch to another app, and the countdown keeps running. The timer shows on the lock screen as a Live Activity, so you can see the time left without opening the app, and you get a notification when a work session or a break ends.

  • 25 minute work sessions with a visible countdown.
  • 5 minute short breaks between sessions.
  • A 15 minute long break after a set of four sessions.
  • Dots that show how many sessions of the set are done.
  • A Live Activity on the lock screen and a notification when each phase ends.

Which habits suit a timer

The timer pairs best with duration habits: habits you track in minutes, such as reading, language practice, writing, studying or an instrument. Set the daily goal in minutes, run sessions until you have done the time, then log the minutes on the habit. The streak counts the day once the goal is met. One 25 minute session covers a goal of 25 minutes, and two sessions with a break between them cover twice that.

Yes or no habits can use the timer too when you want a fixed block, for example a tidy up or a planning session. Run a session, then tick the habit when the block is done.

A simple focus routine

The timer works best with a little structure around it. This routine suits most study, writing or practice habits.

  • Decide on the task before you press start. Starting is easier when you know exactly what the first 25 minutes are for.
  • Put the phone face down once the session is running. The Live Activity and the notification will tell you when it ends.
  • Take the break properly. Stand up, get water, look away from the screen. The 5 minutes are part of the method, not time lost.
  • After the last session, log the minutes on your habit straight away, while the session is fresh.
  • Stop at the end of a set rather than pushing on through the long break. Coming back fresh tomorrow keeps a streak going longer than one heroic day.

If 25 minutes feels long at first, give the habit a goal of one session a day. Once that is steady for a week or two, raise the daily goal and add a second session.

Keeping focus honest with a partner

A timer does not know whether you were working. A partner is harder to fool. Share your study or writing habit with an accountability partner. With Premium, they are told the moment you complete it and can react or nudge you when it is still open in the evening. Without Premium, they see a combined weekly count of what you both completed.

Questions about the focus timer

Where do I find the focus timer?
Open the Today tab and tap the focus timer button. The timer is not tied to a particular habit, so you can use it for any block of focused time.
Can I change the session length?
The timer runs the standard Pomodoro lengths: 25 minute sessions, 5 minute short breaks and a 15 minute long break.
Does the timer log the habit for me?
No. The timer keeps the time and the habit keeps the record. When you finish, log the minutes on your duration habit yourself, and they count toward its daily goal and the streak.
Is the focus timer a Premium feature?
No. The timer is included in the free version.
Does the timer keep running if I leave the app?
Yes. Start a session, switch apps, and the countdown continues. It shows on the lock screen as a Live Activity, and you are notified when a session or a break ends.
